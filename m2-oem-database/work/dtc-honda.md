# Honda / Acura — Manufacturer-Specific DTCs

Research for the M2 OEM vehicle database (v1.2.1). Every factual claim carries a
quality grade: **A** = Honda service manual / Honda TSB · **B** = reputable DTC
database or multi-source agreement (incl. Honda/Acura technician sources) ·
**C** = single community source. SAE J2012 generic codes are NOT duplicated here
(they live in the v1.2 generic catalog). Honda SRS/ABS/VSA modules use proprietary
`x-x` blink-code DTCs rather than standard B1xxx/C1xxx codes — see Quirks.

Research date: 2026-10-06.

## P1259
- Description: VTEC System Malfunction (Rear Bank / Bank 1)
- Category: P
- Scope: manufacturer (Honda)
- Possible causes: low engine oil level; dirty/degraded oil; faulty VTEC solenoid (spool valve assembly); faulty VTEC oil pressure switch; wiring fault to solenoid/switch
- Diagnostic notes: If the code sets at or below ~4000 RPM it tends to be wiring/switch; above ~4000 RPM it tends to be oil-related (Honda master tech guidance). Check oil level and condition FIRST — VTEC is hydraulically actuated. On 1998-2002 Accord the fix was commonly the VTEC spool valve assembly.
- Grade: [B]
- Evidence: Honda DTC lists (description); JustAnswer Honda master technician (RPM split guidance); Fixya owner report (spool valve assembly replacement fixed it); YouTube VTEC test procedure (solenoid/switch/ECM driver tests)

## P1298
- Description: Electrical Load Detector (ELD) Circuit High Input
- Category: P
- Scope: manufacturer (Honda)
- Possible causes: faulty ELD (faulty solder joint per Honda service bulletin — most common); blown fuse; poor connections/loose terminals at ELD or PCM; open in wire between under-dash fuse/relay box and ELD; faulty PCM (rare)
- Diagnostic notes: Honda service bulletin covers this: replace the ELD (P/N 38255-S5A-003); replacement ELDs are now sold separately so the whole under-hood fuse/relay box no longer needs replacement. ELD 3P connector is behind/beneath the under-hood fuse box. Check fuse No. 4 and battery voltage at ELD terminal 1 (BLK/ORN wire) with ignition on.
- Grade: [B]
- Evidence: Honda service bulletin via technician sources (solder-joint cause, part number); JustAnswer Honda dealer techs (diagnostic procedure); multiple owner/technician reports agree

## P1361
- Description: Intermittent Interruption in Top Dead Center (TDC) 1 Sensor Circuit
- Category: P
- Scope: manufacturer (Honda)
- Possible causes: TDC sensor inside distributor (1990s models — replace distributor assembly per Honda); TDC sensor sub-harness damage at firewall side of timing cover (2000s models); poor connections/loose terminals between distributor/sensor and ECM/PCM; faulty ECM/PCM (rare)
- Diagnostic notes: On distributor-era Hondas the TDC sensor is integral to the distributor — Honda recommends the whole distributor. On 2001+ Civic the sensor sits behind the top timing cover (driver side); inspect the 4-pin sub-harness connector and scope the AC sine wave (Green/Red wires for TDC) while wiggling the harness. Can put the engine in limp mode (3500 RPM limit).
- Grade: [B] description; [C] causes (technician forum/field guidance)
- Evidence: Honda DTC lists (description); JustAnswer Honda techs and Car Talk community (distributor vs harness guidance)

## P1362
- Description: No Signal in Top Dead Center (TDC) 1 Sensor Circuit
- Category: P
- Scope: manufacturer (Honda)
- Possible causes: failed TDC sensor; CKP/TDC sensor sub-harness open; distributor ignition housing failure; poor connections/loose terminals between distributor and ECM/PCM; faulty ECM/PCM (rare)
- Diagnostic notes: P1361 = intermittent interruption, P1362 = complete signal loss. Check for metal shavings, oil, or rust inside the distributor (rust indicates worn bearings — replace distributor). Voltage checks: ~12V on BLK/YEL wire, ~5V reference on GRN wire with ignition on.
- Grade: [B] description; [C] causes (technician forum/field guidance)
- Evidence: Honda DTC lists (description); JustAnswer Honda techs (cause list, voltage checks)

## P1456
- Description: Evaporative Emission (EVAP) Control System Leak Detected (Fuel Tank System)
- Category: P
- Scope: manufacturer (Honda)
- Possible causes: loose or faulty fuel filler cap (most common — use OEM Honda cap, tighten 3 clicks); leaking EVAP bypass solenoid; leaking two-way valve; leaking ORVR valve on top of fuel tank; leak in fuel tank or tank-side hoses
- Diagnostic notes: Honda's EVAP self-test runs the tank-side test (P1456) BEFORE the canister-side test (P1457) — a P1456 must be resolved first. Back-probe the fuel tank pressure sensor (GRN wire): ~2.5V at rest; voltage rises as the tank builds pressure with the engine running and should not fall after shutdown. Clamp the hose between the bypass solenoid/two-way valve assembly and the canister to isolate tank-side vs canister-side leaks.
- Grade: [B]
- Evidence: Honda service manual EVAP troubleshooting excerpt via dansprojects.com (code definitions, flowchart); JustAnswer Honda techs (bypass solenoid/two-way valve, pressure-sensor voltages); hondaaccordforum field report (ORVR valve failure; P1456-before-P1457 test sequencing)

## P1457
- Description: Evaporative Emission (EVAP) Control System Leak Detected (EVAP Control Canister System)
- Category: P
- Scope: manufacturer (Honda)
- Possible causes: faulty EVAP canister vent shut (CVS) valve stuck open — most common (valve lives under the car near the rear axle, corrodes); cracked/rotted EVAP hoses; saturated charcoal canister (from habitual fuel top-offs); faulty fuel cap
- Diagnostic notes: Will not affect drivability but fails emissions inspection. A bi-directional scan tool can command the vent (CVS) valve closed while listening for the click; a smoke machine finds the small leaks. Note: the tank-side test (P1456) must pass before the canister-side test runs, so fix P1456 first when both appear.
- Grade: [B]
- Evidence: Honda service manual EVAP excerpt via dansprojects.com; iCarsoft Honda EVAP guide (CVS valve failure mode); multiple technician/owner sources agree

## P1491
- Description: Exhaust Gas Recirculation (EGR) Valve Lift Insufficient Detected
- Category: P
- Scope: manufacturer (Honda)
- Possible causes: faulty EGR valve; clogged EGR ports/passages in intake manifold (carbon buildup — very common on V6 Accord); faulty EGR vacuum regulator/solenoid
- Diagnostic notes: Honda TSB covers the V6 Accord: remove the intake manifold, drill out the EGR port, and install a stainless-steel sleeve kit to prevent carbon re-clogging (goodwill/warranty extension applied on some vehicles). Closed-valve lift-sensor voltage is normally 1.1-1.2V on the WHT/BLK wire; manually applying battery voltage to the valve should swing it to ~4.0V. Use OEM EGR parts.
- Grade: [B]
- Evidence: Honda DTC lists (description); iFixit/JustAnswer Honda techs (carbon-clogged ports, TSB sleeve kit, lift-sensor voltages)

## P1498
- Description: Exhaust Gas Recirculation (EGR) Valve Lift Sensor High Voltage
- Category: P
- Scope: manufacturer (Honda)
- Possible causes: faulty EGR valve/position sensor (note: aftermarket EGR valves have caused repeat P1498); EGR harness connector not supplying 5V reference
- Diagnostic notes: Check the harness connector to the EGR for 5V reference (measured ~5.1V good). If reference voltage is present and the code returns, suspect the EGR valve itself.

## P1607
- Description: ECM/PCM Internal Circuit Failure A
- Category: P
- Scope: manufacturer (Honda)
- Possible causes: failed ECM/PCM (bad solder joints / cracked power rectifier reported); weak battery or charging-system fault (low voltage can set it); corroded/loose ECM/PCM powers, grounds, or connector pins
- Diagnostic notes: Honda procedure: reset the ECM/PCM (disconnect ~5 minutes), ignition ON 40 seconds, recheck. If P1607 returns, update the ECM/PCM software or substitute a known-good unit — if the symptom clears, replace the original. Rule out battery/charging first (cold-weather P1607 that clears when warm points at solder-joint/connection issues). PCM replacement requires immobilizer reprogramming.
- Grade: [B]
- Evidence: JustAnswer Honda/Acura techs quoting Honda procedure (reset/update/substitute sequence); 6thgenaccord and bobistheoilguy field reports (solder joints, power/ground checks, immobilizer relearn)

## B-codes / C-codes (body / chassis)

Honda SRS, ABS, and VSA modules report DTCs in Honda's proprietary `x-x`
blink-code format (e.g. SRS 1-1/1-2, ABS 11-1), NOT as standard B1xxx/C1xxx
codes. No verified Honda-specific B1xxx or C1xxx assignments were found in
this pass — see Coverage notes. Newer models report ISO-format codes with
failure-type bytes (e.g. B0001-1A), which fall under the generic B0xxx range.

---

## Honda quirks

1. **VTEC codes are usually oil codes.** P1259/P2646/P2647: check oil level and
   condition FIRST. The VTEC/rocker-arm system is hydraulically actuated; low
   or dirty oil is the most common root cause, and Honda issued a TSB for the
   oil pressure switch (P/N 37250-PNE-G01) covering 2002-2012 models [B].
2. **P1456 must pass before P1457 runs.** Honda's EVAP monitor tests the fuel-
   tank side first; the canister-side test only runs after the tank side passes.
   Fix P1456 first when both are present [C — owner field report, consistent
   with the test design].
3. **SRS/ABS/VSA use blink codes.** Honda body/chassis DTCs are `x-x` format
   (SRS 5-x = internal SRS unit failure, 11-10 etc.), read via the SCS line or
   HDS — a generic OBD-II reader will not show them [B].
4. **Transmission DTCs blink the D indicator.** Honda A/T and CVT codes set the
   MIL and blink the D indicator; many (P1738/P1739 pressure switches) are
   connector/wiring faults at the transmission before they are mechanical [C].
5. **ELD TSB.** P1298 is usually a faulty solder joint in the ELD; Honda's fix
   is ELD replacement (P/N 38255-S5A-003), now sold separately from the
   under-hood fuse/relay box [B].
6. **P1491 V6 EGR clog.** On V6 Accords the EGR ports carbon-pack; Honda's TSB
   fix is intake removal, drilling the port, and installing a stainless sleeve
   kit [B].
7. **B-CAN codes without a scan tool.** The MPCS short connector on the MICU
   service check connector displays B-CAN DTCs on the odo/trip display [A —
   Honda service manual].
